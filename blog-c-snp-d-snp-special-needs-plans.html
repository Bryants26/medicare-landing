C-SNP vs. D-SNP: Medicare Special Needs Plans Explained

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

Special Needs Plans (SNPs) are a category of Medicare Advantage plan built for people with specific circumstances — not the general Medicare population. Two of the most common types are C-SNPs and D-SNPs. They serve different groups and work differently, even though the names sound similar.

What both types have in common

Both C-SNPs and D-SNPs are Medicare Advantage plans, meaning they replace Original Medicare as your primary coverage and typically bundle in Part D drug coverage. Both are designed to tailor benefits, provider networks, and care coordination around a specific population, rather than offering the same one-size-fits-all structure as a standard Medicare Advantage plan.

C-SNP: Chronic Condition Special Needs Plan

A C-SNP is built for people with specific, severe chronic conditions — such as diabetes, chronic heart failure, or certain lung conditions, depending on which conditions the specific plan covers. To enroll, you generally need documented proof from a provider confirming you have one of the qualifying conditions.

  • Benefits and provider networks are often tailored around managing the specific chronic condition
  • May include care coordination or disease-management support relevant to that condition
  • Formularies are sometimes structured to make condition-related medications more accessible
  • Enrollment requires medical confirmation of the qualifying condition, not just self-reporting

D-SNP: Dual-Eligible Special Needs Plan

A D-SNP is built for people who qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid. Rather than centering on a specific medical condition, eligibility is based on your dual enrollment status. These plans are designed to coordinate benefits between the two programs, often resulting in reduced or $0 costs for many services.

  • Eligibility is based on qualifying for both Medicare and Medicaid, not a specific health condition
  • Often includes broader enrollment flexibility through Special Enrollment Periods throughout the year
  • May include care coordination support that spans both programs
  • Extra benefits vary by plan and by state Medicaid rules
The two aren't mutually exclusive in concept — someone could theoretically have both a qualifying chronic condition and dual Medicare/Medicaid eligibility. But each SNP type is enrolled in based on its own specific qualifying criteria, so it's worth confirming which one (if either) actually applies to your situation.

How to know which might apply to you

  • You may want to look into a C-SNP if: you have a diagnosed, qualifying chronic condition and want a plan built around managing it.
  • You may want to look into a D-SNP if: you already qualify for or are enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid.
  • Neither may apply if: you don't have a qualifying chronic condition and don't have Medicaid — in that case, a standard Medicare Advantage plan or Medigap policy is worth comparing instead.

Availability varies by plan and county

Not every C-SNP or D-SNP is available in every ZIP code, and which chronic conditions qualify for a C-SNP varies from plan to plan and carrier to carrier. It's worth confirming actual availability and eligibility for your specific address rather than assuming based on general information.
