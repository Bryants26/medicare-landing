Turning 65? Here's what to know about Medicare.

A clear, no-pressure walkthrough of your enrollment timeline and coverage choices — from a local, independent agent who starts with your doctors, prescriptions, and budget.

Turning 65 means new choices — Original Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicare Supplement plans, and prescription drug coverage all become part of the conversation. It can feel like a lot at once. This guide breaks down your timeline and options in plain language, and if you'd like help thinking it through, that's what we're here for.

Your Enrollment Timeline

Your Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) is a 7-month window built around your 65th birthday month:

3 months before Birthday month 3 months after

Enrolling at the right point in this window helps you avoid potential gaps in coverage or late enrollment penalties down the road.

Your Main Coverage Choices

Original Medicare (Part A & B)

The federal government's coverage, with the option to add a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan and a standalone Part D drug plan.

Medicare Advantage (Part C)

Private plans that bundle hospital, medical, and often drug coverage into one plan.

Prescription Drug Coverage (Part D)

Worth having in place to avoid a late enrollment penalty — even if you don't take medications yet.

A Simple Turning-65 Checklist

  • Confirm your Medicare card / online account is set up
  • Decide: Original Medicare + Medigap + Part D, or Medicare Advantage
  • Check whether your current doctors and prescriptions are in-network
  • Know your enrollment window so you don't miss it
  • Ask questions before you decide — there's no cost to get help

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to sign up for Medicare when I turn 65?

For most people, yes — enrolling during your Initial Enrollment Period helps you avoid gaps in coverage and potential late enrollment penalties. There are exceptions, such as if you're still covered by an employer group health plan, so it's worth confirming your specific situation.

What happens if I miss my enrollment window?

Missing your Initial Enrollment Period can mean waiting for a later enrollment period and may result in a late enrollment penalty, depending on your circumstances. It's best to plan ahead so you know your dates well in advance.

Is there a cost to talk to an agent?

No. Licensed agents are generally compensated by the insurance carriers, not by you, so there's no cost or obligation to ask questions and compare your options.
