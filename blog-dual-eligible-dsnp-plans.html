What Is a D-SNP? Medicare Plans for People with Medicare and Medicaid

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

If you qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid — often called being "dual-eligible" — there's a specific category of Medicare Advantage plan built around that situation: a Dual-Eligible Special Needs Plan, or D-SNP.

What "dual-eligible" means

Dual eligibility means you qualify for both Medicare (typically due to age or disability) and Medicaid (typically based on income and asset limits set by your state). Texas has its own specific Medicaid eligibility rules, so qualification depends on your individual financial and health circumstances.

What makes a D-SNP different

A D-SNP is a type of Medicare Advantage plan designed specifically for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. These plans are built to coordinate benefits between the two programs, which can mean:

  • Reduced or $0 costs for many services, since Medicaid often covers what Medicare doesn't
  • Care coordination support, sometimes including a dedicated care team
  • Extra benefits beyond Original Medicare, which vary by plan and carrier
  • Help navigating both programs together instead of managing them separately
D-SNPs vary significantly by plan and by state. Not every D-SNP works the same way, and eligibility rules can change, so it's worth confirming your specific situation rather than assuming a plan you've heard about applies to you.

How enrollment works

Because D-SNPs serve a specific population, enrollment rules differ somewhat from standard Medicare Advantage plans — dual-eligible individuals often have more flexibility to make changes throughout the year through Special Enrollment Periods, rather than being limited to the standard Annual Enrollment Period.

Why coordination matters

Without coordination, it's easy for dual-eligible individuals to end up navigating Medicare and Medicaid as two completely separate systems with separate rules, separate cards, and separate customer service lines. A well-matched D-SNP is meant to reduce that friction — though how well it does that varies by plan.
