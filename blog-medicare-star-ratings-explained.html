Medicare Star Ratings Explained: What Do the Stars Actually Mean?

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

Every fall, Medicare Advantage and Part D plans show up with a star rating attached — anywhere from 1 to 5 stars. Marketing leans on this number hard, but most people aren't quite sure what it's actually measuring, or how much weight it should carry in a decision.

What the stars measure

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) rates every Medicare Advantage and Part D contract annually, from 1 star (poor) to 5 stars (excellent), in half-star increments. The rating pulls from dozens of individual performance measures grouped into broad categories — things like how well a plan manages chronic conditions, preventive care rates, member complaints, and customer service responsiveness.

Ratings apply at the contract level, not to a single plan brochure. A single insurance company can offer several plans under one contract, so the star rating you see reflects the whole contract's performance — worth confirming which contract a specific plan actually falls under.

Why the rating matters beyond just marketing

  • Quality bonus payments: Plans rated 3.5 stars or higher receive bonus payments from CMS. Higher ratings mean bigger bonuses, which plans often reinvest into richer benefits or lower costs.
  • 5-Star Special Enrollment Period: If a 5-star plan is available in your area, you can switch into it once per calendar year — outside the normal enrollment windows.
  • A look backward, not forward: A rating reflects performance from an earlier measurement period. It's a snapshot of care already delivered, not a guarantee about next year's network or costs.

5-star plans are genuinely rare

A perfect 5-star rating is uncommon — only a small handful of contracts nationwide achieve it each year, and they aren't available in every state or county. Most well-regarded plans land in the 4 to 4.5 star range, which still represents strong performance and still qualifies for meaningful bonus payments.

The trap: chasing stars over fit

A 5-star rating means a plan performed well across Medicare's quality measures — it doesn't mean it's automatically the best fit for you personally. A 3.5 or 4-star plan that includes your specific doctors and covers your specific medications at a lower cost can be a far better choice than a 5-star plan that doesn't.

Where to check ratings

Star ratings are published on Medicare's official Plan Finder at medicare.gov/plan-compare, updated each fall for the upcoming plan year. It's worth checking the current year's rating directly rather than relying on last year's number, since ratings can shift from one year to the next.
