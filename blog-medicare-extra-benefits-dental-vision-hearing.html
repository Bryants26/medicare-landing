Medicare Extra Benefits: Dental, Vision, Hearing & Fitness

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

One of the most common surprises for people new to Medicare: Original Medicare doesn't cover routine dental cleanings, eye exams for glasses, or hearing aids. Many Medicare Advantage plans fill that gap — but how much they cover varies a lot from plan to plan.

What Original Medicare actually covers here

Original Medicare covers dental, vision, and hearing only in limited, specific medical situations — for example, treatment tied to an accident or a covered surgery. Routine cleanings, glasses, contacts, and hearing aids generally fall outside what Original Medicare pays for.

How Medicare Advantage plans typically fill the gap

  • Dental: Many plans include an allowance for cleanings, X-rays, and sometimes more extensive work like crowns or dentures — with an annual dollar cap that varies widely by plan.
  • Vision: Often includes annual eye exams plus an allowance toward glasses or contacts.
  • Hearing: Often includes a hearing exam and an allowance toward hearing aids, which can otherwise be a significant out-of-pocket expense.
  • Fitness programs: Many plans include a no-cost gym membership program, giving access to participating fitness centers.
These benefits differ enormously between plans — one plan's "dental benefit" might be a basic annual allowance while another includes more comprehensive coverage. The details matter more than the marketing headline.

Are you using what you already have?

If you're already in a Medicare Advantage plan, it's worth checking: are you actually using your dental, vision, and fitness benefits? Unused benefits don't roll over — they simply expire at year's end. Many people are surprised to learn what's already included in their plan that they've never used.

Extra benefits shouldn't be the only factor

These add-ons are worth understanding, but they shouldn't be the deciding factor on their own. A plan with generous dental coverage but a network that excludes your regular doctors may not be the better overall choice. It's worth weighing extra benefits alongside network access, drug coverage, and total costs.
