Your Main Medicare Coverage Choices

Original Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicare Supplement, and Part D — explained plainly, so you can compare with confidence before you decide.

Medicare isn't one-size-fits-all — it's a set of building blocks you put together based on your doctors, prescriptions, and budget. Here's a plain-language look at each piece.

Part A & Part B

Original Medicare

The federal government's coverage, split into two parts:

  • Part A — hospital coverage (inpatient stays, skilled nursing, hospice)
  • Part B — medical coverage (doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services)

On its own, Original Medicare leaves gaps — copays, coinsurance, and no drug coverage — which is why many people pair it with a Medigap plan and a Part D plan.

Part C

Medicare Advantage

Private plans, approved by Medicare, that bundle Part A and Part B — and often Part D — into a single plan. Many include extra benefits like dental, vision, or hearing.

  • Usually works through a network of doctors and hospitals
  • Often has lower monthly premiums than pairing Medigap + Part D
  • Plan details, networks, and costs vary by carrier and county
Medigap

Medicare Supplement

A private policy that works alongside Original Medicare to help cover out-of-pocket costs like copays, coinsurance, and deductibles.

  • Pairs with Original Medicare, not Medicare Advantage
  • Generally lets you see any doctor who accepts Medicare, nationwide
  • Requires a separate Part D plan for drug coverage
Part D

Prescription Drug Coverage

Helps cover the cost of prescription medications. Available as a standalone plan (with Original Medicare + Medigap) or bundled into most Medicare Advantage plans.

  • Worth having even if you take few or no medications now
  • Going without it too long can lead to a late enrollment penalty later
There's no single "best" choice — the right combination depends on which doctors you want to keep seeing, which medications you take, how often you travel, and what fits your budget. That comparison is exactly what a consultation is for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage?

Original Medicare is federal coverage (Part A and Part B) that you can pair with a separate Medigap plan and a standalone Part D drug plan. Medicare Advantage is a private plan that typically bundles hospital, medical, and often drug coverage into one plan with its own network and rules.

Do I need a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plan?

It depends on your health needs, travel habits, and budget. Medigap plans help cover out-of-pocket costs left by Original Medicare, such as copays and coinsurance, in exchange for a monthly premium. It's worth comparing against Medicare Advantage before deciding.

What happens if I don't sign up for Part D?

If you go without creditable prescription drug coverage for too long after you're first eligible, you may face a late enrollment penalty added to your Part D premium later on, even if you don't take medications right now.
