Dual Eligibility

Qualifying for Medicaid in Texas & How to Apply

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

A lot of Medicare beneficiaries assume Medicaid is only for younger, low-income families — and don't realize they may qualify too. If you're 65 or older, or you have a qualifying disability, Texas Medicaid can cover things Medicare doesn't, and in some cases eliminate your Medicare premiums and out-of-pocket costs entirely. Here's how eligibility actually works in Texas, and the exact steps to apply.

Two different Medicaid pathways for seniors

Texas has not expanded Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act, so eligibility for adults is limited to specific groups — seniors, people with qualifying disabilities, and a few other categories. For someone already on Medicare, there are generally two Medicaid pathways worth knowing about:

  • Regular Medicaid for the Elderly and People with Disabilities (MEPD) — health coverage that can work alongside Medicare, with income limits tied closely to the SSI federal benefit rate.
  • Long-term care Medicaid — covers nursing facility care or home- and community-based waiver services, with a higher income limit but its own set of rules.

There's also a separate track worth knowing about: Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB, and QI), which are Medicaid-administered but specifically pay Medicare premiums, deductibles, or coinsurance without providing full Medicaid benefits. If your income is a bit too high for regular Medicaid, you may still qualify for one of these. See the full breakdown of Medicare Savings Programs & Extra Help →

2026 income and asset limits

These figures come from the Texas Health and Human Services Commission and are effective January 1, 2026. They change annually, so always confirm the current numbers before assuming where you fall.

  • SSI Federal Benefit Rate: $994/month — the baseline used for regular MEPD Medicaid income limits.
  • Long-term care / nursing facility income cap: $2,982/month for a single applicant (300% of the SSI Federal Benefit Rate).
  • Asset limit: $2,000 for an individual, $3,000 for a married couple (countable resources — see below for what doesn't count).

Married couples applying for long-term care Medicaid have additional protections for the spouse who isn't applying, allowing that spouse to keep a larger share of income and assets so they aren't left without resources. These "spousal impoverishment" rules are worth a closer look if only one spouse needs nursing home or waiver-level care.

What counts toward the income and asset limits

Income generally includes Social Security, pensions, retirement account distributions, and most other regular payments.

Countable assets generally include bank accounts, savings, investments, and property beyond your primary residence and one vehicle. What typically doesn't count: your primary home (as long as you or your spouse lives there), one vehicle, personal belongings and household furniture, and prepaid funeral arrangements.

If your income is slightly over the limit for long-term care Medicaid, a Qualified Income Trust (sometimes called a "Miller Trust") can, in some cases, allow you to still qualify. This involves a formal legal process and is worth discussing with an elder law attorney rather than attempting on your own.

Basic eligibility requirements

Beyond the income and asset tests, Texas Medicaid generally requires that you:

  • Be a Texas resident
  • Be a U.S. citizen or qualified immigrant (lawful permanent residents generally face a waiting period; refugees and asylees typically do not)
  • Be 65 or older, or meet Social Security's definition of blind or disabled
  • For long-term care Medicaid specifically, meet a medical necessity requirement for nursing facility or waiver-level care

How to apply for Medicaid in Texas

You don't need to guess at eligibility before applying. Applying is free, and a denial doesn't hurt your future eligibility if your situation changes.
  1. Apply online through YourTexasBenefits.com, the state's official portal for Medicaid, SNAP, and other assistance programs. You can also download the Your Texas Benefits mobile app.
  2. Apply by phone or get help screening for eligibility by calling 2-1-1 and selecting the option for Texas Health and Human Services programs.
  3. Apply in person or by mail at your local HHSC benefits office if you prefer paper forms or in-person help.
  4. Gather your documents before you start: proof of age and identity, Texas residency, income (Social Security award letters, pension statements), and asset statements (bank accounts, investment accounts, life insurance policies).
  5. Wait for a decision. Processing timelines vary, but can take several weeks to a couple of months depending on the program and how complete your application is. Respond quickly to any requests for additional documentation to avoid delays.

What if you're denied or think the numbers are wrong?

If HHSC denies your application, you have the right to request a fair hearing, generally within 90 days of the decision. Circumstances also change — an update to your income, assets, or health status can be reason to reapply even after an earlier denial.
