Turning 65 in Texas — What to Do

A step-by-step plan for Texans approaching 65, from a local independent agent. No pressure, no cost to ask questions.

Turning 65 comes with a checklist, not just a birthday. Here's exactly what to do, in order, so nothing slips through the cracks.

Your 6-Step Action Plan

Mark your Initial Enrollment Period

Find your 7-month window: 3 months before your birthday month, your birthday month, and 3 months after. This is your main opportunity to enroll without penalty.

Decide how you'll get Medicare

Choose between Original Medicare (optionally paired with a Medigap plan and Part D) or a Medicare Advantage plan that bundles coverage together.

Check your doctors and prescriptions

Before choosing a plan, confirm your current doctors and medications are covered — this varies plan by plan and county by county.

Compare costs, not just premiums

Look at the full picture — premium, deductible, copays, and out-of-pocket maximum — rather than choosing based on monthly premium alone.

Enroll before your window closes

Submit your enrollment within your Initial Enrollment Period to help avoid coverage gaps or late enrollment penalties.

Review your coverage every year

Plan costs, drug lists, and networks can change annually. Revisit your choice each year during Fall Open Enrollment (October 15–December 7).

A Texas-specific note: Medicare Advantage plans and provider networks vary by county — what's available in Fort Bend County may look different from Harris, Travis, or Bexar County. It's worth checking plans filed specifically for your ZIP code rather than relying on generic statewide lists.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the first thing I should do when I turn 65 in Texas?

Confirm your Initial Enrollment Period dates and whether you need to actively enroll or will be enrolled automatically (for example, if you're already receiving Social Security benefits). From there, decide how you want to structure your coverage.

Does it matter which part of Texas I live in?

Yes. Medicare Advantage plans, provider networks, and even some costs vary by county, so what's available in Fort Bend County may differ from Harris, Travis, or Bexar County. It's worth checking plans specific to your ZIP code rather than relying on general statewide information.

Can I change my mind after I enroll?

Yes, in most cases. Medicare has annual enrollment periods, including Fall Open Enrollment (October 15–December 7), when you can review and change your coverage for the following year.
