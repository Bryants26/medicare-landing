Losing Employer Coverage Mid-Year: Your Medicare Special Enrollment Period

By Bryant Waldron, Licensed Independent Insurance Agent · Serving Richmond, Sugar Land & Fort Bend County, TX

Retirement, a layoff, or an employer dropping coverage can all end your job-based health insurance mid-year — with no regard for Medicare's usual enrollment calendar. If this happens to you at 65 or older, Medicare has a specific Special Enrollment Period (SEP) built for exactly this situation.

How the SEP works

If you delayed Medicare Part B because you had active employer coverage (job-based, through your own employer or a spouse's), you get an 8-month Special Enrollment Period to sign up for Part B without a late penalty. This window starts the month after your employer coverage ends, or the month after your employment ends — whichever comes first.

If you also want to enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan or a stand-alone Part D drug plan, that's a separate, shorter 2-month window that begins once your employer coverage ends.

These two windows run on different clocks and different lengths. It's entirely possible to be inside your Part B SEP but past your window for a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, so don't assume one deadline covers everything.

Timing it to avoid a coverage gap

Waiting until your employer coverage has fully ended before starting the Medicare enrollment process often creates a gap — Medicare enrollment isn't always instant. Most people aim to submit their Part B enrollment the month before their employer coverage ends, so Medicare coverage begins right as the old coverage stops.

COBRA doesn't extend your window

If you're offered COBRA continuation coverage after your job-based coverage ends, it's tempting to think that buys you more time before dealing with Medicare. It doesn't — COBRA is not considered active employer coverage for SEP purposes, and it does not extend your 8-month window. If you're 65 or older, enrolling in Medicare instead of (or before) taking COBRA is generally the safer path.

What to do first

  1. Confirm the exact date your employer coverage ends (get it in writing from HR if possible)
  2. Apply for Part B through Social Security as early as your SEP allows, ideally before coverage actually ends
  3. Decide whether you want Original Medicare with a Medigap policy, or a Medicare Advantage plan, and enroll within your separate 2-month window if applicable
  4. Cancel or let COBRA lapse once Medicare is active, since Medicare typically becomes primary anyway
